SDG17 and PPP projects currently active in Iran

1- United Nations SDG‑17 Partnership and PPP Development Projects

The structural schedule below details the UN Sustainable Development Cooperation Framework (UNSDCF) 2023–2027 and the UNDP Country Programme Document (CPD) 2023–2027 for Iran. The operational cost structures are extracted directly from the formal agreements signed between the UN Country Team, UNDP Executive Board, and the Government of Iran (notably the Ministry of Foreign Affairs). Under this framework, projects operate under National Implementation (NIM) via sovereign line ministries, or Direct Implementation (DIM) via localized private contractors and tech facilitators.

2- FARBOD RAD management team Health Training, Coaching and Treatment Hospital Program

The FARBOD RAD management team Program an integrated cluster of eight (8) world‑class teaching, training, and treatment hospital projects has officially entered into a PPP Funding Facility Agreement in 2025 between the FARBOD RAD management team and MUJTAMEA SAHAMDARAN TOSEA ASIAVE. The approximately value of the development package is 120 million Euro, dedicated to the establishment of advanced medical, academic, pharmaceutical, and rehabilitation facilities in the region.

The entire negotiation, verification, and administrative process of the Agreement was conducted under full monitoring, compliance supervision, and advisory support of the PPP Union. All eight projects have successfully completed their administrative and due‑diligence procedures in accordance with the PPP Union’s regulatory framework, ethical standards, and international PPP compliance protocols.

Both parties have formally confirmed their commitment to implement the projects within a 38‑month timeline. Physical commencement of works will begin once a minimum threshold of regional stability is achieved, ensuring safe, uninterrupted, and ethically responsible execution of all development activities.

Under the PPP Funding Facility Agreement system, the projects are approved as grant‑based development initiatives. The PPP Union affirms that the FARBOD RAD management team Program meets all requirements of international PPP standards, SDG17 partnership principles, transparency obligations, and ethical development criteria.

Following technical evaluation, the PPP Union’s specialist teams have determined that the FARBODA Program will significantly advance the following Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs):

  • SDG 3 – Good Health and Well‑Being
    Expansion of healthcare infrastructure, medical education, and treatment capacity strengthens national health systems and improves public access to quality medical services.
  • SDG 4 – Quality Education
    Establishing teaching hospitals and training centers enhances professional medical education and supports lifelong learning for healthcare personnel.
  • SDG 8 – Decent Work and Economic Growth
    Construction and operation of hospitals generate employment, stimulate the health‑sector economy, and create sustainable professional opportunities.
  • SDG 9 – Industry, Innovation and Infrastructure
    Development of modern hospitals and pharmaceutical centers contributes to resilient infrastructure and promotes innovation in medical technology and clinical practice.
  • SDG 17 – Partnerships for the Goals
    Collaboration between public institutions, universities, private partners, and international facilitators strengthens implementation capacity and reinforces global partnership mechanisms.

“This historic step will be acknowledged as the first successful and coordinated effort by PPP Union members in achieving measurable progress toward the 17 SDGs within the region.”

 3- Mega PPP Projects in Iran

Key Ongoing PPP / BOT / Concession Projects in Iran (Non‑Sanctioned Companies Only)

These projects involve transport, water, energy transmission, and digital infrastructure, and are structured through PPP, BOT, BOOT, or concession models.

1. National PPP Infrastructure Investment Program (2025–2030)

Source: Iran Plan & Budget Organization (Global Investment Iran)

Iran launched a USD 50 billion five‑year infrastructure plan with USD 14 billion reserved specifically for PPP, BOT, and BOOT structures. Key sectors:

  • Transport (rail, ports, roads) – USD 18B
  • Energy transmission & grid – USD 12B
  • Water (desalination, treatment, transfer) – USD 8B
  • Digital connectivity (data centers, backbone) – USD 7B
  • Urban & social infrastructure – USD 5B

Foreign sponsors are explicitly invited through BOT concessions (18 25 years), availability‑payment PPPs, and equity participation in SPVs. This is currently the largest PPP window in Iran.

2. Iran Corridor 2026 Rail & Road PPP Agreements

Source: MarketScreener / IRNA

Iran signed seven major rail and motorway PPP agreements with domestic and foreign investors. Key PPP projects:

  • Rasht–Astara Railway (125 km) Structured with foreign financing and PPP‑style agreements. Includes stations, bridges, grade‑separated crossings.
  • Zahedan Birjand Yunesi Railway (910 km) Preliminary BOT memorandum signed with Pars Railway Development Company. Connects Chabahar port to the national rail network.
  • Chabahar–Zahedan Corridor (Phase II) Part of INSTC; includes PPP electrification and corridor development.

These projects are not linked to any blacklisted companies.

3. Water & Sanitation PPP Projects

Source: World Bank (historic + ongoing frameworks)

Iran continues to operate PPP‑style water projects, including:

  • Northern Cities Water Supply & Sanitation Project
  • Alborz Integrated Land & Water Management Project

Although these were earlier World Bank projects, the same PPP‑BOT frameworks are still used for new water treatment, desalination, and transfer projects under the 2025–2030 plan.

4. Energy Transmission & Grid PPP Opportunities

Source: PPP Fund Overview (P3 Lens)

Iran’s PPP market is expanding in:

  • Power generation (renewables, solar, wind)
  • Grid expansion
  • Transmission corridors
  • Municipal energy efficiency projects

These are structured through concession, BOT, and PPP‑like models under Iranian public works law.

Important: The PPP Fund report explicitly notes private sector participation is growing, and non‑sanctioned local consortia are active.

Important Clarification

Iran does not have a fully centralized PPP authority, but PPP activity is growing through:

  • Ministry‑level concessions
  • BOT contracts
  • PPP Funding Facility Agreements
  • Foreign‑sponsor SPVs
  • Corridor‑based infrastructure packages
  • PPP Union members

This aligns with the PPP Union’s model and SDG17 partnership frameworks

Following the restoration of stability in the region, the PPP Union intends to launch a series of specialized technical workshops delivered by its certified members who possess advanced expertise in SDG‑17 implementation and PPP methodologies. These workshops will provide the private sector with professional guidance, legal orientation, and internationally recognized compliance practices.

The objective of this initiative is to ensure that private‑sector actors are fully equipped with the knowledge, ethical standards, and operational frameworks required to contribute meaningfully to public‑benefit development. Through this structured capacity‑building program, the PPP Union aims to empower private entities to address critical community needs and deliver sustainable solutions across the region by 2030.

This effort reflects the PPP Union’s commitment to responsible governance, transparency, and the promotion of lawful and ethical participation of private‑sector partners in SDG‑aligned development programs.
